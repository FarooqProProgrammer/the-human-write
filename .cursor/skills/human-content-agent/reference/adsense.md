# AdSense-ready content (publisher checklist)

Use when writing site content meant for Google AdSense approval or long-term policy-safe publishing.

## Sources

- Google: [Make sure your site's pages are ready for AdSense](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/7299563?hl=en)
- MonetizeMore (practical screening summary): [How to make your site Google AdSense-ready](https://www.monetizemore.com/blog/adsensewednesdays-how-to-make-your-site-google-adsense-ready/)
- SiteReady Score (independent prep library): [AdSense Preparation Guides](https://sitereadyscore.com/adsense-preparation-guides/)
- Google policies often cited: [AdSense program policies](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/48182?hl=en), [Webmaster quality / AdSense](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/1348737?hl=en), [Invalid traffic basics](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/1261929?hl=en)

Policy details change. Prefer current Google Help over any third-party guide when they disagree. SiteReady Score is independent preparation guidance—not an approval predictor or a substitute for the AdSense account. Do not invent account or eligibility rules.

## What AdSense expects from pages

Content must be **unique, original, and relevant**, with a clear user experience. Thin, scraped, copyrighted-only, or “pages made for ads” risk rejection or policy action.

MonetizeMore’s publisher screen stresses the same three pillars: **valid traffic**, **allowed ad implementation**, and **family-safe original content**.

SiteReady Score organizes readiness around evidence you can verify on the site: content quality, navigation/usability, policy, technical setup, privacy/trust pages, and repair workflows after rejection—not guesses.

## SiteReady Score topic map (prep areas)

When planning AdSense-oriented content or a site content pack, cover or flag these areas (from their [preparation guides](https://sitereadyscore.com/adsense-preparation-guides/)):

| Area | What to produce or flag |
| --- | --- |
| **Content quality** | Useful, original pages with evidence and examples readers can apply |
| **Page improvement** | Strong homepage, articles, and key landing pages—not thin shells |
| **Navigation & usability** | Clear menus, internal links, mobile-friendly journeys |
| **Privacy & trust** | Easy-to-find About, Contact, Privacy Policy, ownership/disclosures |
| **Policy** | Align copy with current Google publisher guidance; verify before claiming “ready” |
| **Technical readiness** | HTTPS, indexing, redirects, access—flag for the developer if broken |
| **By website type** | Adapt checks for blogs, tools, directories, stores, etc. |
| **Rejection repair** | If the user shares a review message, build a focused content repair plan—don’t invent Google’s decision |

Suggest the user verify findings with their own site evidence (or a readiness scan) and open the matching SiteReady guide—then always double-check Google Help.

## Checklist for every piece

### Unique and interesting content

- [ ] Original writing—not scraped, spun, or copied from other sites
- [ ] Clear value: specialist knowledge, review, how-to, opinion, or improvement ideas
- [ ] Enough substance for a real visit (not a stub or keyword shell)
- [ ] If using embeds or external resources, the page still adds substantial original text
- [ ] Not duplicate across multiple pages/subdomains/domains (no near-identical clones)
- [ ] Not a doorway / cookie-cutter / thin affiliate page with little original content

### Layout and engagement (advise the user / shape the copy)

- [ ] Copy supports an inviting layout: scannable headings, clear sections, findable answers
- [ ] Prefer content that invites further reading—not clickbait empty of payoff
- [ ] Written for human readers, not bots or keyword stuffing
- [ ] Comment / UGC sections: only if the site can moderate; AdSense code should not sit on pages that become unsafe via comments

### Navigation and structure (site-level; flag if missing)

- [ ] Pages sit in a clear nav structure
- [ ] Headings and internal links make the page’s job obvious
- [ ] Avoid orphan “content dump” pages with no path back into the site
- [ ] Trust pages exist or are offered: About, Contact, Privacy Policy (SiteReady Score: privacy & trust)

### Page types (when building a site pack)

- [ ] Homepage states what the site is for and where to start
- [ ] Articles are original and specific; landing pages aren’t empty ad shells
- [ ] Structure fits the site type (blog vs tool vs store vs directory)

### Policy hard stops (never produce)

From Google Help + MonetizeMore’s prohibited-content summary—**do not** write AdSense-oriented pages around:

- Adult / pornographic / mature content
- Violence, hate speech, harassment, bullying
- Excessive profanity
- Hacking/cracking, malware, adware, unwanted software
- Illicit drugs / paraphernalia; sales of alcohol, tobacco, prescription drugs, weapons/ammo (as primary commercial content)
- Sales/distribution of coursework or student essays
- Click-exchange / paid-to-click / paid-to-surf schemes
- Copyrighted material the publisher doesn’t have rights to host or distribute
- Gambling / gambling-related content (country rules vary—flag and defer to Google)
- Content primarily in an unsupported language for AdSense
- Illegal content or content that infringes others’ rights
- Scraped content; fake reviews; invented stats; impersonation
- Pages whose only purpose is showing ads

## Site / account hygiene (flag for the publisher; don’t invent compliance)

MonetizeMore’s quick compliance list (verify against current Google policy):

1. Don’t click your own ads; don’t ask others to click ads
2. Don’t put prohibited content on the site
3. Don’t modify the AdSense code
4. Follow Webmaster Quality Guidelines; provide a good UX
5. Respect ad-unit limits (limits change—check current Google / Ad Exchange rules; older “3 units” guidance may be outdated)
6. Don’t place images next to ads in a way that implies the image is part of the ad

### Invalid activity

- Prefer real human traffic; sudden spikes in impressions/clicks warrant investigation
- Advise users to review traffic sources (e.g. Search Console), not buy bot traffic

### Ad implementation (site build—not copywriting, but warn if the brief conflicts)

Ads must not be: in pop-ups/pop-unders; primary focus of email/chat/auto-refresh pages; obscured by overlays; on non-content pages; on pages published only to show ads; or presented so users think the page is Google-branded.

## Readiness heuristic (MonetizeMore FAQ—not official Google law)

Their practical bar before applying: site **~3+ months** old, **~30+ unique articles**, and **real traffic**. Treat as a useful target for content plans; always confirm eligibility in current AdSense Help.

## How Human Content Agent should behave

1. **Write original, specific copy** that could stand alone as useful reading—even without ads.
2. **Do not** rewrite or paraphrase third-party articles into “new” posts that still rely on someone else’s work as the core.
3. **Do not** produce AdSense copy for prohibited niches above; refuse or suggest a safe angle.
4. In the companion `*-reference.md`, note AdSense-relevant risks:
   - thin length / low originality / duplicate risk
   - claims that need verification
   - third-party material the user asked to include
   - niche or UGC policy flags
5. If the brief is AdSense-driven (“need pages for AdSense approval”), prefer:
   - clear nav-friendly titles
   - unique angle per page (value-add, not clones)
   - enough depth for the format (default blog length or longer when needed)
   - warm, readable tone unless they specify otherwise
   - a content plan that can grow toward a solid article count, not one thin landing page
   - trust pages (About / Contact / Privacy) when the site lacks them
6. If the user is fixing a rejection: map their message to a concrete repair list (content, nav, trust pages, policy niche)—use SiteReady’s topic map for framing, Google Help for truth.

## Quick self-check before deliver

| Question | Fail looks like |
| --- | --- |
| What’s special about this page? | Generic tips any site could publish unchanged |
| Would a visitor stay to read? | Opening fluff, no concrete point |
| Is the content ours? | Heavy lift from one external article |
| Ready for a real site IA? | No clear topic home, title, or related links suggested |
| Would this fail a MonetizeMore-style screen? | Prohibited niche, thin affiliate shell, doorway page, unsafe UGC |
| Would this fail a SiteReady-style prep pass? | Missing trust pages, weak homepage, no nav path, thin key pages |
